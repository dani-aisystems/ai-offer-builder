# Deck design and copy

Standing preferences for how an offer deck looks and reads. Read this before Phase 4 (deck plan) and again before Phase 5 (build). They come from repeated review feedback on real decks. A rule here is a default: a client's content can justify a deviation, but say so in the plan.

## Follow a proven reference deck

When the user supplies a reference deck (a previous offer that worked), it is the template, not inspiration. Preserve its slide sequence, grid, margins, spacing, component hierarchy (cards, accent bars, eyebrows, price blocks), typography scale, comparison-table convention and information density. Change only what the client's content requires: copy, prices, images, palette, fonts, and any slide the plan adds on purpose. Do not redesign "to improve it".

Copy the structure and the system, never the reference client's name, facts, prices, wording or photos. The reference deck's name must not appear in the deck, its notes or its file properties.

## Design principles

- **One system.** Every slide uses the same grid, margins, card style, corner treatment, accent motif and type scale. A new slide reuses an existing component (for example the four-card layout) rather than inventing one.
- **Restrained and deliberate.** Leave space. Do not fill empty areas with decoration. Every shape, rule and icon has a job; remove it if it does not.
- **Clear hierarchy.** One dominant element per slide (a title, a price, a number). Supporting text is visibly secondary.
- **Short copy on slides.** A card holds a heading and one or two short lines. Detail goes to the offer record and speaker notes.
- **Check marks for included features** in comparison tables (a real ✓ glyph), and the reference deck's convention (usually a dash) for not included. Do not use dots or decorative bullets as inclusion markers.
- **Cover.** Prefer the client's logo on the cover over a venue or interior photo: it is brand-safe and raises no photo-rights question. Keep the logo's aspect ratio; give a wide logo a panel that suits its shape instead of stretching it.
- **Images.** Only real photos and logos, never distorted, never a picture of a slide. Use an image only when it adds information or atmosphere the client can stand behind.
- **Palette and fonts from the client's visuals** (logo, photos, existing site), in the reference deck's layout. The agency stays visible as the sender: its logo, name and contact on the closing slide and in the footer. If the client has no usable visual direction, use the agency's own look.
- **Install the fonts.** After choosing fonts, check they are installed, tell the user which files need installing on the presenting computer, and keep copies with the deck's working files. A symbol missing from the brand font (✓, →) is set in a font that has it.
- **Contrast is computed** for every text and background pair, including text on tinted cells, badges and dark slides.
- **Size text for the wider renderer.** PowerPoint and Keynote set text wider than LibreOffice, Cyrillic and other non-Latin text most of all. Size boxes for text about 12 to 14 percent wider than the preview shows.

## Copywriting rules

- **No em dashes.** Not in slide text, tables, speaker notes, document properties or the offer record. Use a comma, colon, period, parentheses or a rewritten sentence. (A dash meaning "not included" in a comparison table is an en dash or hyphen, never an em dash.)
- **Short, specific, owner-friendly.** Plain business language in the client's language, addressed the way the reference deck addresses the reader (for example the formal "you" where the language has one). Say what the thing does for the business.
- **Headlines are short enough for one or two lines** at their font size. A title that wraps to a lone word is rewritten, not shrunk.
- **No buzzwords or AI-sounding filler**: "elevate your digital presence", "seamless", "cutting-edge", "unlock", "leverage". If a sentence survives replacing the client's name with another's, it is generic.
- **No repetition across slides.** The same insight in new words on an adjacent slide is a defect (see the layer table in `offer-principles.md`).
- **Confident, not exaggerated.** Promise deliverables, not outcomes. State estimates as estimates and assumptions as assumptions, on the slide.
- **Terminology is consistent.** One name per package, per page, per feature, everywhere. Package names match across package slides, comparison, recommendation, next steps and the record.
- **Wrapping is part of the copy.** Reword so each bullet is one line or two full lines; no line ends in a lone short word or number. Fix by rewording first, then spacing, then font size, in that order.
- **Only true facts.** Never invent statistics, customer numbers, revenue, traffic, reviews or history. Facts the client owns (rating, hours, prices) come from the user or a source you opened.
